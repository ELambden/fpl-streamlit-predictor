Why a Random Forest Helps

FPL decisions are rarely decided by one clean rule. Random Forests help by combining many small judgement calls into one balanced estimate.

Think of it as a room of scouts

One scout cares most about minutes. Another is cautious about injury flags. Another looks at xGI. Another notices fixture difficulty. A Random Forest builds many simple decision trees like that, then averages their opinions.

It handles awkward tradeoffs

A cheap defender with nailed minutes and good fixtures is a different bet from a rotation attacker with exciting xGI. A straight-line formula struggles with those bends. A forest can learn that the same signal matters differently depending on the rest of the profile.

It reduces overreaction

One haul can distort judgement. The model looks at rolling points, minutes, starts, xGI, ICT, price, ownership, team context, and fixture difficulty together. That makes it harder for one noisy stat to dominate the recommendation.

What it still cannot know

The model does not read press conferences, tactical leaks, private purchase prices, or late team news. Its job is to narrow the decision space and make tradeoffs visible. Final FPL decisions still need human judgement.
